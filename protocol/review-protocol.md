# Pilot review protocol

## Objective

Map the evidence for acute and post-acute withdrawal after sustained amphetamine or
methamphetamine use while preventing silent extrapolation between substances and outcomes.

## Primary question

Among adults who cease or substantially reduce sustained non-medical amphetamine or
methamphetamine use, what is the time course of symptoms and functional recovery, and which
interventions improve withdrawal-specific outcomes?

## Primary outcomes

- depression or dysphoria;
- anhedonia and motivation;
- fatigue and energy;
- sleep duration, quality, and circadian disruption;
- anxiety, irritability, and psychomotor change;
- attention, memory, executive function, and cognitive speed;
- craving;
- suicidality, psychosis, and psychiatric destabilization;
- social and occupational functioning;
- quality of life;
- return to use and treatment retention;
- adverse events.

## Mandatory distinctions

- direct amphetamine evidence versus direct methamphetamine evidence;
- prescribed discontinuation versus non-medical use;
- withdrawal relief versus reduction in use;
- reduction in use versus functional recovery;
- statistical significance versus clinically meaningful benefit;
- absence of evidence versus evidence of no effect.

## Pilot status

This release is an evidence-map prototype. A full systematic review would require registered
protocol refinement, exhaustive searches, duplicate screening, duplicate extraction, formal
risk-of-bias assessment, and human adjudication.
