# Measurement, psychiatric overlap, and attribution

(claim-c10)=
## C10 · A scale is not a universal adaptor

**Claim.** Withdrawal measurement instruments are not automatically portable across cocaine,
methamphetamine, and non-methamphetamine amphetamine populations, so scale validity should be
checked in the target substance group.

The Amphetamine Withdrawal Questionnaire was developed with initial reliability, validity, and
factor-structure evidence in an amphetamine-labelled withdrawal population
[@srisurapanont1999a]. Later studies used it in methamphetamine cohorts
[@mcgregor2005]. The Stimulant Selective Severity Assessment performed acceptably in cocaine and
cocaine-plus-other-stimulant groups, but internal consistency was lower in the primarily
methamphetamine-only subgroup and the authors advised against using it there
[@walker2019].

Meta-analyses inherit this problem when they combine different withdrawal scales, time points,
and substances [@sharafi2024; @acheson2023]. A numeric score may look crisp while the construct
underneath it is wobbling.

(claim-c11)=
## C11 · Withdrawal and psychiatric conditions need parallel timelines

**Claim.** Withdrawal symptoms and co-occurring psychiatric conditions should be assessed
concurrently and repeatedly because stimulant-induced symptoms, rebound phenomena, sleep
deprivation, and independent disorders can overlap and change at different rates.

ASAM/AAAP guidance emphasizes concurrent assessment and management of psychiatric risk,
including psychosis, depression, and suicidality [@asam2024]. The BAP guideline similarly
recommends integrated monitoring rather than waiting for every substance-related symptom to
disappear before addressing comorbidity [@sinclair2026]. The clinical withdrawal review
foregrounds this diagnostic overlap [@li2023].

Longitudinal data illustrate the timing problem: average psychosis and depression can improve
faster than craving [@zorick2010], while retrospective amphetamine reports include mood,
irritability, pain, and social impairment [@cantwell1998]. A single assessment cannot reliably
label every symptom as withdrawal, relapse of an underlying condition, or both.

## Safety-critical variables for future extraction

- suicidal ideation and behavior;
- psychosis, agitation, and paranoia;
- bipolar-spectrum activation or depression;
- ADHD diagnosis and prescribed stimulant exposure;
- sleep deprivation and circadian disruption;
- alcohol, benzodiazepine, cannabis, opioid, and other stimulant use;
- medication changes;
- nutrition, housing, violence exposure, and access to care.

This pilot maps the need for those variables. It does not diagnose them.
